Vodacom Engage

From a wall of tiles to a tool.

Read the story
ClientVodacom HRIT, Midrand
RoleSenior Product Designer
IndustryTelecommunications
Year2024

Vodacom's employee app holds communications, policies, wellness, campus services and rewards for the whole group. It opened on a wall of tiles with no search, so people gave up before they found what they came for. I ran the redesign as senior product designer: research, information architecture, navigation and testing. Post-launch task testing put discoverability of key features up by about 60%.

Feature discoverability up ~60% after launch.

The Vodacom employee engagement app is the front door for everything people need at work — communications, policies, wellness, campus services, rewards, strategy content and linked apps. The original home screen opened with a branded GIF loader, a scrolling communications banner and then a long, flat wall of tiles. Everything was there. Almost nothing was findable.

My objective was to rebuild the navigation and information architecture so employees could get to a feature, an article or an app in as few steps as possible — and to make the app feel like a tool they choose to open rather than one they have to dig through.

Client work delivered with Vodacom HRIT. Some internal detail has been generalised or omitted to respect confidentiality.

The problem

Everything was there. Almost nothing was findable.

01Key features were buriedCritical tools sat several scrolls deep in an undifferentiated tile grid. Employees often gave up before reaching the thing they opened the app for.ImpactHigh-value features were effectively invisible, while the same handful of top tiles absorbed almost all engagement.
02No way to searchThere was no search across features, articles or tools. Users had to remember where something lived, and recall-based navigation fails fast in a large organisation.ImpactRoutine tasks turned into support calls and "where do I find…" messages to HR and IT.
03The banner competed with the workA scrolling communications banner pushed the useful content below the fold and mixed announcements in with navigation, so users could not tell what was action and what was news.ImpactAnnouncements were read, features were not used — the opposite of what the product needed.
04Nothing was personalisedThe app looked identical for a technician on a plant floor, a line manager and an HR administrator. No favourites, no recents, no shortcuts to what each person actually uses.ImpactNo reason to come back — once the novelty wore off, only mandated actions brought people in.

Goals

Fewer taps to the thing people opened the app for.

BUSINESS GOALS

What the business needed

  • Increase feature discoverability and reduce the number of taps required for common tasks.
  • Cut internal support volume caused by employees not locating features or content.
  • Raise voluntary engagement so the app becomes a channel HR can rely on, not just a compliance tool.
USER GOALS

What people needed

  • Find a feature, policy or tool in seconds — by searching rather than guessing where it lives.
  • See the things I personally use most, right on the home screen.
  • Understand the difference between news, tools and updates without having to test everything.

Role & responsibilities

Product Designer.

UX Research, Information Architecture, Wireframing, Prototyping, Usability Testing, UI Design

Our users

Three people, three very different working days.

Research combined employee feedback from existing users with a review of how comparable employee-engagement apps handle navigation and discoverability. Three behavioural groups shaped the design decisions.

~60%improvement in discoverability of key features in post-launch task testing.

Personas

PERSONA 01

The Frontline Employee

Works shifts, opens the app in short bursts on a mid-range Android phone, usually while standing up. Needs policies, payslips, wellness and shift tools — fast.

Jobs To Be Done

When something changes on site, I want to pull up the right policy or form in a few seconds from my phone, so I can answer a supervisor or a colleague without walking to a kiosk.

PERSONA 02

The Line Manager

Uses the app to push communications and check team-related content. Cares whether people actually see what the team is being told.

Jobs To Be Done

When a message matters, I want to know it was easy for my team to find and read, so I am not chasing people individually.

PERSONA 03

The Service Desk Administrator

Spends the day resolving "where is this" questions. Watches the support queue and knows exactly which features people cannot find.

Jobs To Be Done

When the same question arrives three times in a week, I want the interface itself to answer it — so my queue stays focused on real problems.

Process

Research first, pixels last.

4 stages — Research & Analysis → Wireframing & Information Architecture → High-Fidelity Design & User Testing → Iteration, Implementation & Launch.

01 · Research & Analysis

Research & Analysis

I gathered feedback from existing users who had repeatedly reported difficulty navigating the app, and reviewed comparable employee engagement products to see how they handled grouping and discoverability. The pattern across all of it was the same: users do not want more tiles, they want fewer decisions.

02 · Wireframing & Information Architecture

Wireframing & Information Architecture

Low-fidelity wireframes mapped the new flow first. Tiles were grouped into meaningful sections — Safety, Health and Wellbeing; Policies and Information; Smart Buildings; Rewards and Benefits; Company Strategy; and Apps. A dedicated search with filters was designed to reach features, content and tools directly, a chatbot section surfaced every available bot in one place, and a favourites row was placed below the communications banner so people could pin what they actually use.

03 · High-Fidelity Design & User Testing

High-Fidelity Design & User Testing

High-fidelity mockups were built and tested with employees. Testing focused on three things: how fast someone could locate a known feature, whether search results matched expectation, and whether the favourites concept was understood without explanation. The profile icon gained a floating menu showing personal detail, app information and usage, and the chatbot section was reworked with icons to make it scannable.

04 · Iteration, Implementation & Launch

Iteration, Implementation & Launch

Feedback from stakeholders and testers drove small but important refinements to visual hierarchy and consistency. I then worked directly with the development team through implementation — layout, responsiveness and interaction detail — and reviewed builds against design before release.

Post-launch, analytics and user feedback confirmed the direction: search became the dominant entry point, the grouped sections replaced tile-hunting, and favourites gave people a reason to return without being told to.

Impact

Validated before rollout, measured after launch.

The redesign was validated in usability testing before rollout and tracked after launch. Discoverability of key features improved by roughly 60% in post-launch task testing, time-to-task for the most common journeys dropped by about a third, and "where do I find…" support requests fell noticeably once search and grouped sections shipped.

More importantly the app stopped behaving like a notice board and started behaving like a workspace — search became the primary entry point, and the favourites row became the second most used element on the home screen.
