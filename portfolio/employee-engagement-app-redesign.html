Vodacom Engage

The group employee app had everything in it, and almost none of it could be found. I rebuilt the navigation and information architecture so people reach a feature, article or app in fewer steps.

The problem

Vodacom Engage is the front door for communications, policies, wellness, campus services, rewards and linked apps. It also carries the landing-page carousel, push notifications, surveys, rewards, wellbeing content and FAQs that employees rely on. The original home screen opened with a branded loader, a scrolling communications banner and a long, flat wall of tiles.

  • Critical tools sat several scrolls deep in an undifferentiated grid, so people gave up before they reached what they opened the app for.
  • There was no search across features, articles or tools, so people had to remember where something lived.
  • Routine questions turned into support calls and "where do I find…" messages to HR and IT.
  • The banner pushed useful content below the fold and mixed news in with navigation.

My role

I was the senior product designer on the redesign. I led the research, the information architecture, the navigation, and the usability testing, and I worked with the development team through implementation, reviewing builds against the design before release.

Research

I gathered feedback from existing users who had repeatedly reported difficulty navigating the app, and reviewed comparable employee-engagement apps for how they handled grouping and discoverability. Three groups shaped the decisions:

Shift worker
Opens the app in short bursts on a phone, usually standing up. Needs policies, payslips, wellness and shift tools quickly.
Line manager
Uses the app to push communications and check team content. Needs to know a message was easy for the team to find and read.
Service desk administrator
Spends the day answering "where is this" questions. Needs the interface to answer them instead.

Decisions

1. Group the tiles and add one search

Tiles were grouped into sections, including Safety, Health and Wellbeing; Policies and Information; Smart Buildings; Rewards and Benefits; Company Strategy; and Apps. A single search with filters reaches features, content and tools directly. A chatbot section lists every available bot in one place.

2. Put favourites where people look first

A favourites row sits below the communications banner, so people can pin what they actually use. The banner stayed, but it no longer pushes the useful part of the screen out of view.

3. Test the concepts before the build

High-fidelity mockups were tested with employees on three questions: how quickly someone could find a known feature, whether search results matched their expectation, and whether favourites made sense without explanation.

Process

1. Research and analysis
User feedback and comparable apps.
2. Wireframes and IA
Grouped sections and search.
3. High fidelity and testing
Find-time, search and favourites tests.
4. Iteration and launch
Refinements and build review.

Outcome

The redesign was validated in usability testing before rollout and tracked after launch. Search became the main way into the app, the grouped sections replaced tile-hunting, and favourites became the second most used element on the home screen.

~60%better discoverability of key features in post-launch task testing ~⅓less time to complete the most common journeys

"Where do I find…" support requests fell noticeably once search and the grouped sections shipped.
