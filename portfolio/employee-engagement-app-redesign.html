Enterprise mobile app · Product design

Vodacom Engage

The group employee app had everything in it, and employees could not find most of it. I rebuilt the navigation and information architecture so people reach a feature, article or app in fewer steps.

RoleSenior product designer
DatesOct 2021 – Mar 2023
EmployeriOCO, embedded with Vodacom HRIT
TeamVodacom HRIT stakeholders, with the design and development team

Context and stakes

Vodacom Engage is the front door for communications, policies, wellness, campus services, rewards and linked apps. It also carries the landing-page carousel, push notifications, surveys, rewards, wellbeing content and FAQs employees rely on.

The home screen opened with a branded loader, a scrolling communications banner and a long, flat wall of tiles. There was no search, so people had to remember where something lived. Employees gave up before they reached what they opened the app for, and routine questions became calls and "where do I find…" messages to HR and IT.

What would have happened if nothing changed: the app would stay a notice board. Only mandated actions would bring people back, and support load would keep rising.

My role and boundaries

I was the senior product designer on the redesign. I led the research, the information architecture, the navigation and the usability testing. I worked with the development team through implementation and reviewed builds against the design before release.

The development team built the interface; I reviewed the builds against the design before release.

Three decisions

1. Group the tiles and add one search

Options considered: I reviewed comparable employee-engagement apps for how they grouped and surfaced features. keep the single grid and add a "most used" row.

Chose: sections such as Safety and Wellbeing, Policies and Information, Rewards and Benefits, and Apps, with one search across features, articles and tools.

Trade-off accepted: search helps people who know what they want, but browsing gets harder for people who do not. Grouping helps discovery, but it adds sections to maintain.

2. Put favourites below the banner

Options considered: a banner-first layout with favourites hidden in a menu.

Chose: a favourites row below the communications banner, so people can pin what they use, and the banner no longer pushes the useful part out of view.

Trade-off accepted: the banner has less room on screen.

3. Test concepts with employees before the build

Options considered: launch without user testing and rely on post-launch feedback.

Chose: test high-fidelity mockups with employees on three things: how quickly someone finds a known feature, whether search results match expectation, and whether favourites make sense without explanation.

Trade-off accepted: testing took time before the build could start.

Outcome and evidence

The redesign was validated in usability testing before rollout and tracked after launch. Search became the main way into the app, the grouped sections replaced tile-hunting, and favourites became the second most used element on the home screen.

~60%better discoverability of key features in post-launch task testing, against the old home screen measured in post-launch task testing ~⅓less time to complete the most common journeys measured in post-launch task testing

"Where do I find…" support requests fell noticeably once search and the grouped sections shipped.
