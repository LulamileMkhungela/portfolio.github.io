The problem

Toyota's ownership experience lived in call centres and dealer desks. The owner app needed servicing, bookings, finance and support in one place, and the remote app needed to tell owners what a lock or climate command actually did.

My role and boundaries

  • Designed the owner journeys in Figma and built them in Ionic and Angular.
  • Ran UX research across 10+ African markets with Hotjar and turned findings into a prioritised backlog tied to Toyota KPIs.
  • Mentored four junior designers through research framing and component critique.
  • Paired with the Angular team on implementation reviews to close the gap between the Figma spec and the shipped UI.

Three decisions and what I traded away

A state model for remote commands

Each lock, climate or tracking command shows whether it is done, pending or failed, so an owner knows what happened.

Bookings against real dealer availability

Owners book servicing against live dealer slots instead of a phone call.

One token set across Toyota, KINTO and Lexus surfaces

The same tokens and components were used across the programme so the apps read as one brand.

Process

Research across markets
Owner journeys in Figma
Build in Ionic/Angular
Implementation review

One system across Toyota, KINTO and Lexus

The owner apps, the KINTO brand programme and the Lexus South Africa app share one token set and one component library, so an owner moving between them sees the same behaviour and the same states.
