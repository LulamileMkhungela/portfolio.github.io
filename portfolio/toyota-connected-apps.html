Toyota Connected Apps

Toyota's ownership experience lived in call centres and dealer desks. I designed and built the owner app and the remote-control app on one design system shared across the Toyota programme.

RoleSenior UI/UX designer and Ionic/Angular developer
DatesAug 2023 – Apr 2025
EmployeriOCO, for Toyota South Africa
ScopeUX research, Figma design, Ionic/Angular build

Context and stakes

Servicing, bookings, finance and support lived across call centres and dealer desks. Owners who wanted to book a service had to phone, and owners of connected cars had no clear sense of whether a remote command had worked.

Two apps needed to fix this: the owner companion (today's MyToyota) and Toyota Remote for lock, climate, vehicle status and tracking. Both had to work across several African markets.

What would have happened if nothing changed: owners would keep phoning dealers, and remote commands would keep giving unclear feedback.

My role and boundaries

I designed the owner journeys in Figma and built them in Ionic and Angular, so the design and the shipped app were the same work. I ran UX research across 10+ African markets with Hotjar, turned the findings into a prioritised backlog tied to Toyota's KPIs, and mentored four junior designers through research framing and component critique. I also paired with the Angular team on implementation reviews.

Three decisions

1. Show what a remote command actually did

Options considered: a single success message once the command was sent.

Chose: each command shows whether it is done, pending or failed, so an owner knows what happened.

Trade-off accepted: more states to design, test and keep consistent across markets.

2. Book servicing against real dealer availability

Options considered: a request form that the dealer confirms by phone.

Chose: appointments made against real dealer availability, without a phone call.

Trade-off accepted: the booking is only as good as the dealer slot data behind it.

3. One design system across Toyota, KINTO and Lexus

Options considered: each product team styles its own app.

Chose: the same tokens and components across the programme, so the apps read as one brand.

Trade-off accepted: individual teams have less freedom to deviate from the shared system.

Outcome and evidence

The work was research-led, system-built and shipped across markets. MyToyota carries the owner companion forward, Toyota Remote handles connected-car controls, and the Lexus South Africa app is the connected ownership companion built on the same system.
