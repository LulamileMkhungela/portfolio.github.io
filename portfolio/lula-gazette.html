LulaGazette puts South African law in one place and opens on the page: 71 curated documents across six content types, each with a citation, a plain-language summary and the official PDF one tap away. It is a Next.js 14 App Router application — search route, reader, two audience modes, a workspace and a coverage page that publishes its own gaps. I designed the interface and built the app. The live link is a static deployment of the library shell; the full build runs from the repository.

A legal library anyone can search — before they sign up.

LulaGazette brings six kinds of South African legal material into one interface: Acts of Parliament, reported judgments, court forms, court rules, regulations and provincial laws. Every record carries an official citation, a plain-language summary, a date and a status, plus the source PDF where I have one — 42 of them ship with the build.

The product is a Next.js 14 App Router application written in TypeScript and Tailwind, with react-pdf for the reader. I built the search route, the document reader, the audience modes, the workspace, the coverage page and the status page.

Everything here is checkable: the live link opens the library shell as deployed, the repository holds the full build, and /coverage and /legal/status state what works, what is still in progress and what I do not claim.

The product

Six content types, one reading experience.

01

Acts

The Constitution of 1996, the Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995, the BCEA 75 of 1997, POPIA 4 of 2013, the Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008, the National Credit Act 34 of 2005 and the Companies Act 71 of 2008 — each with a summary and a citation you can copy.

02

Cases

Reported judgments with their real citations: S v Makwanyane [1995] ZACC 3, Grootboom 2000, Olivia Road 2008, Blue Moonlight 2011, Sidumo 2007, PE Municipality 2004, Treatment Action Campaign 2002.

03

Forms and rules

The paperwork a matter actually starts with: CCMA Form 7.11, Form N1, the magistrates' court summons, the Uniform Rules and the Magistrates' Courts Rules — filed against the right institution.

04

Regulations & provincial laws

POPIA regulations and the 2025 commencement notice, the national minimum wage schedule, the Gauteng liquor regulations, KwaZulu-Natal planning and Western Cape liquor — the layer below the Act.

05

Search that keeps its state

One route, /s, carrying query, category, country, audience, sort, licence and year range in the URL. Country defaults to ZA, an unknown code falls back rather than emptying the page, and guests can search before they register.

06

Cited overviews

The overview engine returns a headline, a plain-English position, next steps, warnings, citations and a confidence band. It is retrieval over the local corpus with topic routing — not a free-text model — and it refuses to answer without a source.

The decision

Open the library first.

The decision I am proudest of: search is not gated. Anyone can search, read a summary, open the PDF and copy the citation before creating an account. Sign-in is for saved matters, bookmarks and the workspace — never for a search box.

The challenge

Good law, fragmented access.

South African legal material is free but scattered: statutes on one site, judgments on another, forms on a third, provincial notices in a gazette most people have never opened. I wanted one place where a citizen, a student and a practitioner could start.

01Sources are distributedStatutes, judgments, rules, forms and regulations live on different sites in different formats.
02Jurisdiction is part of the information architectureA South African answer is not an African answer. Country is a first-class filter, not a footnote.
03A citation has to survive the clickA summary is only useful if the reader can reach the page it came from and check it.
04Coverage is not currencyA library can hold a statute and still be out of date. The product separates the two and says which one it is showing.
05An answer without a citation is not an answerLegal information that cannot be traced back to a source is a rumour with better typography.

Inside the build

What is actually in the repository.

01

The route set

/s search · /doc/[id] and /d/[id] readers · /library · /c/[category] · /countries/[code] · /courts · /directory · /guides · /templates · /lawyers · /workspace · /account · /admin · /coverage · /sources · /legal/status.

02

Two audiences, one corpus

Individual and Lawyer modes share the data and switch the navigation, the visible records and the copy — audience filtering happens at query time, not by maintaining two libraries.

03

The reader

42 official PDFs bundled with the build, opened in the app with react-pdf and pdf.js, page counts from a metadata file, and the extracted gazette text beside the page it came from.

04

The overview engine

One function composes the answer from documents, guides, courts and templates, applies twelve topic routes (dismissal, eviction, privacy, credit, bail, equality), and returns citations, warnings and a confidence level with the text.

05

Coverage that names its gaps

The coverage page counts documents, categories, countries and connectors straight from the data, then lists the three gaps I will not paper over: point-in-time consolidation, paywalled reports and sub-national by-laws.

06

The source registry

25 sources in one file — SAFLII, the Government Gazette, the Constitutional Court, Kenya Law, LawNigeria, GhanaLII, the regional courts, the CCMA, the Information Regulator — each with a role and a connector flag that the hub and the coverage page both read.

Stated plainly

The scraper layer, honestly.

/api/scrape and /api/pull return a source-aware corpus, filtered by source, country, query and audience; scripts/scrape_all_sources.mjs writes a per-source run report to src/data/harvest_report.json. What is not wired yet is an HTTP crawl of the publishers themselves. So the connector list is a contract I can build against — it is not proof that the library updates itself, and I have not published a freshness measure.

Product goal

Make jurisdiction part of the search.

My goal was narrow: let someone land on the site, pick a jurisdiction and a content type, find the right document and open the page. I have not measured whether that makes research faster for practitioners — I have only built the path and made it inspectable.

54jurisdiction records, each with its gazette, its apex court and its court rules — plus a pan-African entry for regional bodies.

The solution

Ship the shortest path to a correct answer.

Search, read, copy the citation, open the source.

  • 01A search route that holds its state: query, category, country, audience, sort, licence and year range live in the URL, so a result set can be sent to someone else and arrive intact.
  • 02A reader that shows the document and the page: 42 bundled PDFs opened with react-pdf, page numbers from metadata, and the extracted gazette text beside the scan.
  • 03Two audience modes over one corpus — citizen rights and everyday guides for individuals, pleadings, court procedure and authority bundles for practitioners.
  • 04Overviews that cite: every generated answer returns its sources, its warnings and a confidence level, and the topic routing runs on the local corpus rather than a browser-side model call.
  • 05A workspace with saved matters and bookmarks, and a status page that separates what works, what is in progress and what is deliberately not claimed.

Product decisions

Three choices that shaped the product.

01

Default to South Africa, and say so

A missing country param resolves to ZA, an unknown code falls back instead of emptying the page, and “all” is the only explicit way out. The default is visible in the filter, not hidden in the code.

02

Keep the citation in the same breath as the summary

Every card carries its citation and a copy action. A summary you cannot check is a summary you should not rely on.

03

Publish the limits on the same site

/coverage lists the gaps and /legal/status says what is in progress. The limits are product pages, not a footnote under the footer.

The live link

Trade-offs

01

Breadth before depth

I put 54 jurisdictions on the map before going deep in one. Coverage is uneven, and the coverage page says so rather than averaging it into a headline number.

02

Corpus before crawl

The scraper layer serves a source-aware corpus and a connector contract. Crawling the publishers is the next step; until it is wired, the library does not claim to be current.

03

Open the search box first

Anyone can search before signing in. Accounts are for saved work, matter notes and the workspace — not for the library.
