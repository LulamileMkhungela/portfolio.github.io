SnB Chartered Accountants

Answers before the first call.

Read the story
ClientSnB Chartered Accountants & Auditors
RoleUI/UX & Android Developer, Sesyme & SmartServe
IndustryProfessional services
Year2019

SnB's first client call was being spent answering questions a website should answer. In 2019, while I was a UI/UX and web designer at OpenSesyme in Mthatha, the firm came to us needing exactly that fixed. I took the work from the first meeting to launch: content audit, structure, design, build — and a site the firm could keep current itself.

A site that answers what clients ask before they call.

SnB is a 100% black-owned network of firms founded in 2015, operating as SnB Chartered Accountants & Auditors for assurance work and SnB Advisory Services for accounting, taxation and advisory. It serves SMEs, NPOs and public sector entities from three offices — Johannesburg, Mthatha and Pongola — with a team drawn from firms including the Big 4.

The old site said "Partnership ^ Quality ^ Efficiency" and little else a prospective client could act on. Audit, accounting and taxation were listed as headings; what was missing was who the work is for, what an engagement actually covers, and how to start one.

I redesigned the site around the questions clients ask before they appoint an auditor — scope, credentials, offices, and what happens next — and rebuilt it so the firm can maintain it itself.

The work moved from discovering the questions callers already asked, to defining what the site had to answer, structuring the service information, designing the responsive pages, then checking the contact route and handing the CMS over. Done from a desk in Mthatha, with the client's Mthatha office a short drive away.

Client work designed and hand-coded by LulaMile while at OpenSesyme, Mthatha.

The problem

The services were named, not explained.

01The services pages named the work without explaining itAuditing, accounting and taxation were headings with bullet lists, never described in terms of client type, scope or process.ImpactProspects with a real need could not tell whether the firm was right for them, so they phoned to ask — or went elsewhere.
02Credibility was claimed, not shownA firm with 50+ clients, 12+ service lines, three offices and Big 4 experience presented none of it in a way a visitor could absorb.ImpactThe firm competed on price instead of on track record.
03The sub-services clients actually search for were buriedExternal audit, independent reviews, agreed-upon procedures, bookkeeping, payroll, tax opinions — all of it existed, none of it was findable.ImpactHigh-intent searches landed on pages that did not answer them.
04The firm could not update its own siteContent changes needed a developer, so prices, service lines and office details drifted out of date.ImpactThe site slowly stopped matching what the firm actually offered.

Design strategy

Show the credibility. Explain the work. Let the firm run it.

BUSINESS GOALS

What the business needed

  • Make the site the first qualified conversation, before the first call.
  • Present scale and credentials as evidence, not decoration.
  • Let the firm update services, people and offices without a developer.
USER GOALS

What people needed

  • Understand what the firm does, for whom, and how an engagement starts.
  • See the credibility — clients, service lines, offices — without asking.
  • Reach a person quickly if the matter is urgent.

UI/UX & Web Designer at OpenSesyme.

Design process

Opening the firm's file before drawing the site.

For this Mthatha firm, the first research artefact was already being kept by the person answering the phone.

01 · DiscoverWeek 0–1

Start with the questions callers already ask

What must a prospective client know before deciding whether to appoint the firm?

The Mthatha receptionist kept a handwritten list of what new callers asked: are you registered, do you do payroll, where are your offices? I inventoried the old pages, reviewed the competing options clients considered, and mapped those questions to the enquiry path.

In practice: Content inventory · competitor review · enquiry-path mapping

02 · DefineWeek 1

Define what the site must answer

Visitors needed to understand the firm's services, credentials, offices and what happens after they make contact.

The strategy kept the firm's own “Partnership ^ Quality ^ Efficiency” line, then made the offer concrete: who the work serves, what each service covers, and how an engagement starts.

In practice: Problem statement · business goals · audience needs

03 · IdeateWeek 1

Turn the questions into a useful structure

Organise services and proof in the order a client needs to understand them.

The sitemap followed the questions people arrived with: audit, accounting and taxation, each with its own sub-services. Page briefs set the priority order around who the firm is, what it does, proof of scale and how to start. Partners reviewed the wireframes before the visual design was finished.

In practice: Information architecture · sitemap · page briefs

04 · DesignWeek 1–2

Make credibility the visual language

Design a mobile-first interface that makes the firm's identity and evidence easy to scan.

High-fidelity design used generous whitespace, legible type and real numbers—50+ clients, 12+ services and three offices—where they supported a decision. The two entity logos made the structure of the firm legible before a paragraph did.

In practice: Wireframes · high-fidelity interface design · mobile-first layout

05 · Testing & deliveryWeek 2–3 · Launch in Week 3

Check the route, then hand it over

Make sure the service pages and contact route answer the original questions, and leave the firm able to keep them current.

I built the site responsively, routed the enquiry form to the right person, and set up a CMS the administrator could update. The live pages and contact route were checked against the discovery questions; tracking and analytics supported follow-up after launch.

In practice: Responsive build · contact-route check · CMS handover · analytics

Impact

The questions before an appointment, answered.

The site now answers the questions that precede an appointment: what each service covers, who it suits, how the firm works, and what happens next. Service pages carry the sub-services clients actually search for — external audit, independent reviews, agreed-upon procedures, internal audit; bookkeeping, management accounts, financial statements, payroll; tax computation, returns, opinions, compliance.

The numbers are treated as proof rather than decoration: 50+ clients, 12+ services and three offices are stated where they support a decision, and the firm maintains its own content through a CMS, so the site stays current through a busy audit season.

Trade-offs

01

Make credibility the visual language

A number-led design can read as a sales page for a conservative firm. To confirm: how the firm reacted to the visual approach.

02

A number-led site the firm can keep current

Every figure on the site has to be kept current by the firm. To confirm: who maintains the figures and how often.
