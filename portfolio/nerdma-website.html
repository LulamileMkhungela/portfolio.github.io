Nerdma

Explain the ecosystem, not the software.

Read the story
ClientNerdma Systems
RoleDevsigner, freelance (independent, after hours alongside iOCO)
IndustryGovernance, risk and sustainability intelligence
Year2023

Nerdma Systems sells five products that only make sense together, and the old site listed them like software on a shelf. In 2023, as a freelance UI/UX designer working after hours alongside my iOCO role, I led the redesign around the buyer's real decision: clarify the problem, structure the product story, design evidence-first interfaces, then take the work through delivery.

A site that explains an ecosystem instead of listing software.

Nerdma Systems sells governance, risk and compliance intelligence to serious buyers — multinational groups, listed corporates, government entities, NPOs and SMEs — through a suite of products: NerdScore, NerdRisk, NerdTech, NerdSmart and NerdGrid.

The old site listed the products but never explained the ecosystem. A technical evaluator could not tell how the products relate, and a decision-maker could not tell what they were buying or how a rollout would work.

I designed the marketing site that leads with the positioning line the business already owns — "One Ecosystem. Sustainability-Driven Intelligence." — gives each product its own presence, and states the methodology plainly. Alongside the site I designed the product dashboards — NerdScore, NerdGrid and NerdGrow — where the same data has to work for an analyst on screen all day and for an executive who needs the position in one view.

The work moved from discovering the gap and defining each buyer's decision, through a clearer product structure and interface design, into delivery and post-launch measurement. The dashboards were designed in parallel so the marketing site never promised something the product could not show.

Independent freelance engagement, designed and built by LulaMile.

Design strategy

Qualify the technical buyer. Convince the one who signs.

BUSINESS GOALS

What the business needed

  • Explain the ecosystem so a technical and commercial buyer can both qualify it.
  • Use certifications, partners and client base as first-class proof.
  • Give the product dashboards a structure that scales as the data grows.
USER GOALS

What people needed

  • Understand what each product does and how they work together.
  • See the position at a glance, then drill into what caused it.
  • Export or present a view without rebuilding it by hand.

Design process

Diagnose the disconnect, then tell one platform clearly.

The product dashboards were designed alongside the site, so its story matched what the product could show.

01 · DiscoverWeek 0–1

Two buyers, one broken story

Who is evaluating the platform, what each buyer must decide, and what the old site leaves unexplained.

The existing site named NerdScore, NerdRisk, NerdTech, NerdSmart and NerdGrid without showing how they worked together. I inventoried product and credibility content, reviewed competing GRC platforms, and mapped the enquiry path to locate the gap between the offer and a buyer's decision.

In practice: Content inventory · competitor review · enquiry-path mapping

02 · DefineWeek 1

Make the decision clear

Technical evaluators needed product fit and rollout clarity; executives needed the position and its implications at a glance.

That became the problem statement and design strategy: explain how the products formed one ecosystem, make each product's role legible, and bring evidence—methodology, partners and certifications—into the buying journey.

In practice: Problem statement · business goals · audience needs

03 · IdeateWeek 1

Give the ecosystem a structure

Organise the products around the questions visitors bring, not as a flat feature list.

The sitemap placed the five products under the positioning the business already owned—“One Ecosystem. Sustainability-Driven Intelligence.”—then gave methodology, partners, certifications and client proof a deliberate place. Page briefs made the relationships clear before interface design began.

In practice: Information architecture · sitemap · page briefs

04 · DesignWeek 1–2

Evidence-first interfaces

Design the marketing site and dashboards so the same story carries from promise to product.

Wireframes and high-fidelity design gave the site a dark, technical visual language built around evidence. NerdScore, NerdGrid and NerdGrow dashboards brought the primary score, its trend and contributing factors into a clear reading order.

In practice: Wireframes · high-fidelity interface design · dashboard hierarchy

05 · Testing & deliveryWeek 2–3 · Launch in Week 3

Ship, measure, improve

Check the responsive experience and enquiry route, then use live performance to guide the next iteration.

I built the site responsively, mapped old URLs, connected forms to the enquiry pipeline, and checked content editing and performance. After launch, page performance and enquiry quality informed the iteration backlog.

In practice: Responsive build · redirects and forms · post-launch analytics

Impact

The ecosystem, explained in one scroll.

The site now explains the ecosystem in one scroll: the positioning line, the five products with their individual roles, the methodology as four named stages, and the credibility — certifications, partners and a client base from multinationals to government — presented as content rather than buried.

The NerdScore, NerdGrid and NerdGrow dashboards were structured the same way: one primary number per view, the trend behind it, then the contributing factors, with a consistent table and filter grammar across all three so learning one teaches the others. What used to be exported to a spreadsheet is now readable on screen, and the same layouts hold as more sites, assets and periods are added.
