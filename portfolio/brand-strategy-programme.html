Toyota Brand Programme

Three products, one brand.

Read the story
ClientToyota South Africa · via iOCO
RoleSenior UI/UX Designer & Ionic/Angular Developer, via iOCO
IndustryAutomotive & mobility
Year2023 — 2025

Toyota South Africa was taking three mobility products to market at the same time, and each one was drawing its own identity as it went. I was the senior designer on the brand programme: I set the positioning, built the identity system, and wrote the rules for using it, so KINTO, the Toyota App and Toyota Remote could launch in the same voice.

One brand system across product, campaign and retail — launches stopped rebuilding identity.

Mobility services in the Toyota ecosystem grew product by product: KINTO for subscription and sharing, the Toyota App as the customer front door, and Toyota Remote for connected-car control. Each had been launched with its own visual language, its own tone and its own idea of what the customer should feel.

I worked on the brand side of that portfolio — positioning, the identity system that holds the brands together without flattening them, and the application of that system into product interfaces, campaign and retail.

The work followed the brand process end to end: strategy and positioning, identity system, application, then guidelines and rollout.

Public brand and product work delivered with Toyota Motor Corporation.

The problem

Every product spoke its own language.

01Every product spoke its own languageThree customer-facing expressions of the same business, with different type, colour, tone and even different words for the same thing.ImpactCustomers could not tell they were dealing with one company, and each product paid to earn trust from scratch.
02Positioning was a product listEach brand explained what the service did, not who it was for or why it mattered against the alternatives.ImpactSales conversations restarted at the beginning every time, in every market.
03Identity could not survive productBrand assets stopped at the marketing site. Inside the app, the interface was designed separately, so nothing carried through.ImpactThe brand existed in campaigns and disappeared the moment a customer logged in.
04Launches rebuilt the brand each timeWith no shared system, every new service started its identity work from zero.ImpactRepeated cost and inconsistency, with each launch slower than it needed to be.

Goals

A system that survives contact with product.

BUSINESS GOALS

What the business needed

  • Position each mobility brand clearly against its own alternatives.
  • Build one identity system that scales across product, campaign and retail.
  • Make a new launch an application of the system, not a reinvention.
USER GOALS

What people needed

  • Understand what the service is and who it is for, immediately.
  • Recognise the same company across app, campaign and dealership.
  • Trust it with a subscription or a connected-car service.

Process

Strategy first, then identity, then everywhere.

4 stages — Strategy & positioning → Identity system → Application → Guidelines & rollout.

01 · Strategy & positioning

Strategy & positioning

Audience work, competitive framing and a brand platform for each service: who it is for, what it promises, what it is not, and the language that carries it. Positioning was agreed in writing before any visual work started.

02 · Identity system

Identity system

The identity was built as a system rather than a logo: wordmark and lockups, colour with defined roles, a type family with a hierarchy, spacing and layout rules, iconography and motion — all specified so different teams reach the same result.

03 · Application

Application

The system was applied where customers actually meet it: product interface tokens and components, campaign and social templates, and retail dealership materials. Applying it in product first is what stopped the brand disappearing after the login screen.

04 · Guidelines & rollout

Guidelines & rollout

A brand book with the decisions and the reasoning, agreed usage rules, and rollout sessions with marketing, product and retail so the system could be used without me in the room. Adoption was tracked by auditing live assets against the guidelines.

Impact

The same tokens in the app, the campaign and the showroom.

The brands now share a positioning framework and an identity system — type, colour, spacing, tone and motion — that survives contact with product: the same tokens are applied in the app as in campaign and retail.

Because the system is documented and applied, a new service launches as an application of the brand rather than a new identity project, which is the difference between weeks and quarters. The brand book, tokens and rollout guidance are used by marketing, product and dealers.
